# Netflix Business Insights Report

## Key Insights
- **Catalogue mix.** Movies are 69.7% of the catalogue. The TV Show share of yearly additions rose from 29.5% in 2017 to 33.7% in 2021 while movie additions fell from 1,422 (2019) to 993 (2021, partial year).
- **Geographic concentration.** The United States alone holds 38.1% of titles with a known country and the top 5 countries hold 66.2%. Diversification depends on a small set of markets.
- **Audience targeting.** Adult-rated content is 45.6% of the catalogue versus 23.4% for Kids and Older Kids. Family content is the smallest rated segment.
- **Market formats differ.** Pakistan is series-led (83% TV Shows) while India is movie-led (8% TV Shows), so the two largest South Asian markets need different content plans.
- **Content format.** Median movie runtime is 98 minutes and 67% of TV Shows have a single season, so most series are short runs or limited series.
- **Freshness.** 55% of titles were added within a year of their release, and 85% of the catalogue was released after 2009 (release-year peak: 2018, 1,144 titles).
- **Data quality.** 29% of titles have no director and 3% have no country, which limits creator-level and market-level analysis.

## Recommendations
1. Evaluate expanding family and kids programming, the smallest rated audience segment in the catalogue.
2. Replicate the series-led approach seen in Pakistan, South Korea and Japan in other emerging markets.
3. Reduce dependence on the US and India by growing mid-size markets such as Spain, Mexico, Egypt and Nigeria.
4. Track TV Show seasons per title to measure retention and plan renewals for single-season series.
5. Make country and director fields mandatory at ingestion to improve downstream analytics.
